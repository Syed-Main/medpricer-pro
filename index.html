Actuarial Memorandum: Portfolio Performance & Remediation

Prepared for the Pricing Committee & Chief Underwriting Officer

Context: A rigorous claims experience and rate adequacy analysis was performed across the Sample Medical Book (representing $42.5M GWP, 15,000 covered lives, UY 2022–2024). The book currently operates at a 71.6% incurred loss ratio and a 98.0% combined operating ratio, leaving underwriting margins compressed well below the target 5.0% profitability threshold.

To restore the portfolio to a sustainable 65.0% break-even loss ratio for FY 2025 renewals, an overall portfolio rate increase of +8.9% is indicated, alongside targeted structural adjustments to benefit tiers and age rating curves.

1. Cross-Subsidization & Structural Underpricing in Senior Cohorts (Ages 50–64)

The 50–59 cohort is running at 78.0% LR and the 60–64 cohort is at 85.2% LR, driven by a 14.2% surge in inpatient surgical frequency and chronic oncology therapies. The existing age relativities (2.45x and 3.10x) understate the actual empirical severity gradient.

Recommendation: Steepens age relativities to 2.65x (50–59) and 3.40x (60–64). Restricts unrated family add-ons for dependents above age 60.

2. Platinum Tier Moral Hazard & First-Dollar Utilization

The zero-deductible Platinum tier produces a 78.9% LR ($4.18M claims on $5.30M premium). Outpatient consultation frequency is 2.8x higher than the Bronze baseline, confirming moral hazard under first-dollar coverage.

Recommendation: Introduce a mandatory $250 outpatient co-pay or 10% prescription co-insurance on Platinum renewals. Shift corporate quote default to Gold tier.

3. Medical Trend Acceleration Exceeding Pricing Assumptions

Observed medical inflation across network hospital contracts ran at 7.8% p.a. over the past 18 months, compared to the 5.0% trend baked into prior underwriting tariffs. Compounded over the 1.5-year pricing horizon, this creates a 4.1% pricing shortfall before any utilization changes.

Recommendation: Lock in a 7.8% trend factor into prospective rate cards and introduce semi-annual hospital tariff indexing in large corporate accounts.
